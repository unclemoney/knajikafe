Grammar Notes
1. The causative form of verbs
The causative form of Japanese verbs is formed by attaching “せる” or “させる” to the verb stem. The specific conjuga-
tion rules are as follows:
Group I (godan) verbs:
Change the final u-row kana of the verb to the corresponding a-row kana and attach “せる”.
Note: verbs ending in “う” first change to “わ” before attaching “せる”. For example:

返す：かえす→さ＋せる＝返させる

書く：かく→か＋せる＝書かせる
う う

よ よ
読む：よむ→ま＋せる＝読ませる
よ よ
呼ぶ：よぶ→ば＋せる＝呼ばせる
おく おく

使う：つかう→わ＋せる＝使わせる

2. Japanese causative sentences I: transitive causative sentences
Causative sentences express making someone do something, allowing someone to do something, etc.; they can be translated as “let/make ... (some-
one) do ... (something)”. According to the transitive/intransitive distinction, Japanese causative sentences fall into “transitive causative
sentences” and “intransitive causative sentences”. The basic pattern of a “transitive causative sentence” is:
Transitive causative sentence: N は/が N に N を V（さ）せる
N is the causer, serving as subject marked with “は” or “が”; N is the causee (the doer of the action),
marked with the particle “に”; N is the direct object of the action, marked with “を”. For example:
てんいん ほん なら
① a. 店員が本を並べます。／The shop assistant arranges the books.
ほんや しゅじん てんいん ほん なら
b. 本屋の主人が店員に本を並べさせます。／The bookstore owner has the shop assistant arrange the
books.
てんいん なら
Example ①a is an active sentence: the doer of the action, “店員”, is the subject, and “並べる” is the action of the subject.
ほんや しゅじん
In contrast, example ①b is its causative sentence: here “本屋の主人” becomes the subject who makes the assistant arrange the
てんいん
books, while the doer “店員” becomes the causee in the “transitive causative sentence”,
なら ほん
marked with “に”; the direct object of the action “並べる” is “本”, marked with “を”. It is worth
noting that Japanese causative sentences are mostly used for a superior causing a subordinate to act. When a subordinate asks a superior to do something,
the causative form is generally not used.

3. Japanese causative sentences II: intransitive causative sentences
Intransitive causative sentences express making someone do something, allowing someone to do something, or causing someone to feel a cer-
tain emotion, etc.; they can be translated as “let/make ... (someone) ... (do something)”. When the causative verb in a Japanese causative sentence is in-
transitive, the doer of the action can be marked with “を” or “に”. The basic pattern is:
Intransitive causative sentence: N は/が N に/を V（さ）せる
N is the causer, serving as subject marked with “は” or “が”; N is the causee, and also
the doer of the action, marked with “を” or “に”. It is worth noting here that, when the doer's will
is disregarded and coercion is expressed, “を” is usually used (as in example ①); when the doer's will
is considered and permission is implied, the doer can be marked with “に” (as in example ②).

6. V てはじめて
The te-form of a verb followed by “てはじめて” describes that only after the experience or event of the first clause
does one gain a new realization or understanding of the content described in the second clause. The first clause is mostly a precondition, and the second is mostly some-
thing that has not happened, or a rule, truth, etc.
In addition, the second clause of the “てはじめて” pattern can also be in the past tense, used to describe that only after the experience of the first clause
or the event occurred did one realize or understand something previously unnoticed or ungrasped.
