Grammar Notes
1. V ないでください。 ＜negative request (prohibition)＞
“V ないでください” is the negative form of the request pattern “V てください”, and is mostly
used to express prohibition. It can be translated as “please don't ...”, “do not ...”, etc.
す
① ここではタバコを吸わないでください。／Please do not smoke here.
あぶ い す のぼ
② 危ないですから、椅子に登らないでください。／It's dangerous, so please don't climb on the
chair.
しゃしん と
③ 写真を撮らないでください。／Please do not take photos.
2. V てもいい ＜permission＞
A verb followed by “てもいい” expresses permission for (the other party) to do something, equivalent to “you may
....” When “V てもいい” is used as a question, the subject is usually the first person (“わたし”) — the speaker
themselves — and is often omitted, equivalent to “may I ...?” Also, the “いい” in the pattern “V
だいじょうぶ
てもいい” can sometimes be replaced with “大丈夫”.
ほん か
① A：この本を借りてもいいですか。／May I borrow this book?
B：ええ、どうぞ。／Sure, go ahead.
らいしゅう かのじょ つ
② 来 週 のパーティーに彼女を連れてきてもいいですよ。／You may bring your girlfriend to next week's
party.
し りょう いんさつ だいじょう ぶ
③ A：こちらの資 料 を印刷しても大 丈 夫ですか。／May I print this mate-
rial?
だいじょうぶ
B：はい、大丈夫ですよ。／Yes, that's fine.
3. V なくてもいい ＜not necessary＞
The verb nai-form followed by “なくてもいい” expresses permitting someone not to do something, translatable as “you don't
have to ...”, “it's all right not to ....”
かえ
① 返さなくてもいいです。／You don't have to return it.
おも ほん あし た も
② 重い本は明日、持ってこなくてもいいですよ。／You don't have to bring the heavy books
tomorrow.
いそ きょ う き
③ 急ぎませんので、今日は決めなくてもいいですよ。／There's no rush, so you don't have to decide
today.

4. V てはいけない ＜prohibition＞
A verb followed by “てはいけない” expresses prohibition, translatable as “must not ...”, “you are not allowed to
...”. It is mostly used for requirements from superiors to subordinates, or to state rules and discipline.
こ ども よるおそ そと あそ
① 子供は夜遅くまで、外で遊んではいけないよ。／Children must not play outside until late at night.
としょかん の もの つくえ うえ お
② 図書館では飲み物を机の上に置いてはいけません。／In the library, you must not
leave drinks on the desk.
た もの としょかん も こ
③ 食べ物を図書館に持ち込んではいけません。／Food must not be brought into the library.
5. V なければならない ＜have to; obligation＞
The verb nai-form followed by “なければならない” means “must”, “have to”. It expresses
the necessity or obligation to do an action. Patterns with similar meanings to “V なければならない” include:
“V なくてはいけない”, “V なければいけない”, and “V なくてはな
らない”. In spoken language, “V なければ” can be shortened to “V なきゃ”, and “V なくては” can be short-
ened to “V なくちゃ”.
に ほんじん いえ はい とき くつ ぬ
① 日本人の家に入る時、靴を脱がなければなりません。／When entering a Japanese
person's home, you must take off your shoes.
に ほん うし ざ せき し
② 日本では後ろの座席もシートベルトを締めなければいけません。／In Japan, seatbelts must be fastened even in the back seat.
やす しごと お ざんぎょう
③ せっかくの休みですが、仕事が終わらないので、残業しなければなら
ないんです。／It's a rare day off, but because the work isn't finished,
I have to work overtime.
6. V ることができる ＜able to＞
The verb dictionary form followed by “ことができる” expresses “being able to (do something).” Here,
“able” can mean either the ability to do something or having the conditions to do it, translatable as
“can ...”, “be able to ....” For example:
ご はな
① ドイツ語を話すことができますか。／Can you speak German?
か あし た ほうこく
② レポートを書いたので、明日みんなに報告することができます。／Since I've finished writing the report, I can present it to everyone tomorrow.

7. N だけ + particle ＜limitation＞
“だけ” can attach to a noun to express limitation, translatable as “only”, “just”, etc. Depending on the
needs of the sentence, “だけ” can be followed by particles like “で”, “に”, “は”, “を”, “が”; when followed by “を” or
“が”, those particles can be omitted.
に ほんじん こ ども ひら が な さくぶん か
① 日本人の子供たちは平仮名だけで作文を書きます。／Japanese children write compositions using only hiragana.
② これだけはやめてください。／Just this one thing — please don't do it.
かね きょ う ひる はん す
③ お金がないので、今日のお昼ご飯はサンドイッチだけで済ませます。
／Since I have no money, I'll make do with just a sandwich for lunch today.
Expression Expansion
1. けっこうです。
In this lesson, “けっこうです” means “that's fine”, expressing approval by someone with the authority
to decide of another person's words or actions. Note that when expressing approval, “けっこうです”
cannot be used toward someone of higher status.
“けっこうです” can also be used to decline someone, translatable as “no thank you; I've had enough.”
When expressing refusal, “けっこうです” is a fairly polite expression and can also be used toward someone of higher sta-
tus. Which meaning “けっこうです” has must be judged from the context and the speaker's
tone. For example:
こうちゃ すこ
① A：紅茶をもう少しいかがですか。／Would you like some more tea?
B：もうけっこうです。／No, thank you (I've had enough).
あした こ
② A：明日は来なくてもいいですか。／Is it all right if I don't come tomorrow?
B：はい、けっこうです。／I'm fine, thanks.
さそ
③ せっかくのお誘いですが、わたしはけっこうです。／I appreciate the kind
invitation, but I'll pass this time.
