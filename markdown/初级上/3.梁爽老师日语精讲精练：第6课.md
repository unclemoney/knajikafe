Grammar Notes
1. ko-so-a-do demonstratives
The ko-so-a-do words are four groups of demonstratives beginning with こ, そ, あ, and ど respectively,
used to point to things, places, or directions (see Table 6-1), translatable as "this ...", "that ..."
"that ... (over there)", and "which ...". Although both "so-series" and "a-series" words can be rendered "that ...",
they must be distinguished in actual use.
When the speaker and listener are far apart, the speaker uses "so-series" words for things or directions closer to the
listener. "A-series" words generally refer to third-party things or directions far from both the speaker and the
listener.

Interrogative どれ どこ どちら

4. N (place) に N が あります／います。 <existence sentence I>
This is the basic pattern of the "existence sentence". The particle に follows "N (place)" to mark the
"place of existence", like "in/at ..." in English. The particle が marks the subject that exists (N). The verbs あ
ります and います both mean "to exist", like "there is/are". Their negative
forms are ありません and いません. あります is mostly used for things that cannot move on their own;
います is used for animate beings such as people or animals that can move on their own.

いっかい
① 1階にコンビニがあります。／There is a convenience store on the 1st floor.
きょうしつ がくせい
② 教室に学生がいます。／There are students in the classroom.
えき ちか なかむら
③ 駅の近くに中村さんのアパートがあります。／Near the station is Nakamura's apartment.

5. N (place) には N が あります／います。 <existence sentence II>
The particle は marks the topic. Generally, a Japanese sentence can take a part other than the predicate verb
and promote it to the topic of the sentence by adding the topic-marking particle. In the basic pattern "N (place) に N が
あります／います。", to make the place where something exists the topic of the sentence,
add the topic particle は after "N (place) に", giving the pattern:

N (place) には N が あります／います。 <existence sentence II>
けいたい
① かばんには携帯があります。／There is a phone in the bag.
となり や
② コンビニの隣にはパン屋があります。／Next to the convenience store is a bakery.
6. N は N (place) に あります／います。 <existence sentence III>
To make the thing that exists (i.e., the subject) the topic of the sentence, replace the subject particle が
with the topic particle は, giving the pattern:
N は N (place) に あります／います。 <existence sentence III>
① あのう、Wi-Fiはありますか。／Excuse me, is there Wi-Fi?
ゆ か へ や さんがい
② 由加さんの部屋は、３階にあります。／Yuka's room is on the 3rd floor.
"N は N (place) に あります／います。 <existence III>" can also be interchanged with the previously learned
"N は N です" pattern, mostly in spoken style. Compared with "〜は〜にありま
す／います", "N は N です" simply omits the existence verb ありま
す or います, but the meaning is basically the same.
びょういん
③ −1 A：あのう、病院はどこですか。／Where is the hospital?
ぎんこう まえ
B：銀行の前です。／In front of the bank.
びょういん
③−2 A：あのう、病院はどこにありますか。／Where is the hospital?

7. それから
"それから" is a conjunction. As an independent word, a conjunction generally connects preceding and following sentences,
paragraphs, or words. "それから" generally has two meanings: one follows a preceding item and indicates
that the following item happens next, usually translated "then"; the "それから" in this lesson
expresses addition, equivalent to "also; in addition" in English.
けいたいばんごう
① それから、これはわたしの携帯番号です。／Also, this is my phone number.
② それから、これ、ほんの気持ちです。／And also, this is just a small token.
なかむら り こばやし だいがくいんせい
③ 中村さん、李さん、それから、小林 さんは大学院生です。／Nakamura,
Li, and also Kobayashi are graduate students.

2. Ways to end a conversation
Japanese people generally don't use "さようなら" when saying goodbye, because it has the nuance of
"farewell forever". Japanese people choose an appropriate way to end a conversation depending on the context. Here, using the patterns from this lesson
as examples, we explain a few common situations.
つか さま
（１）お疲れ様でした。
つか さま
This is a common Japanese greeting meaning "thank you for your hard work". お疲れ様でした is the past
form, used to show appreciation after someone has worked hard. Note that this greeting is generally not used
toward people of higher status. When it comes up in conversation, you can reply like this:
つか さま
① A：お疲れ様でした。／Thanks for your hard work.

<!-- Page 11 -->

つか さま
B：お疲れ様でした。（used between people of similar age or standing）／Thanks for your hard work.
つか さま
② A：お疲れ様でした。／Thanks for your hard work.
B：ありがとうございました。（used toward someone of higher status）／Thank you.
やす
（２）ゆっくり休んでください。
やす やす
"ゆっくり" is an adverb meaning "slowly; at leisure". "休んでください" is made of the verb 休む
(to rest) plus the auxiliary てください meaning "please (do)". The ね at the end of the sentence
is a modal particle. The sentence means "please get some good rest".
やす
Of course, if it is at night before bed, you can also use "お休みなさい"
(good night), learned in Lesson 1.
（３）ありがとうございました。
"ありがとうございました" is the past tense of "ありがとうございます", and is also
a common greeting for parting on formal occasions. Japanese people generally don't say "さ
ようなら" when parting, because "さようなら" is too formal and can imply "farewell forever". Instead,
you can end the conversation with "ありがとうございました", carrying the sense of "thank you very much for today".

3. Common expressions for giving small gifts
In daily work and life, Japanese people have the custom of giving small gifts to those who look after them.
These gifts are usually small local specialties. Japanese people are very reserved when expressing feelings,
き も
often saying "ほんの気持ちです" while handing over the gift. It means "(this is) just a little
token of my appreciation".
ゆ か き も
① 由加：これ、ほんの気持ちです。／This is just a small token (of my thanks).
なかむら
中村：わざわざすみません。／Sorry to have made you go to the trouble.
