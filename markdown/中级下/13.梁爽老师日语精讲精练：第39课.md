Grammar Notes
1. Honorific language and special honorific verbs
The Japanese honorific system is vast and complex, involving many parts of speech such as verbs, nouns, and adjectives. As for honorific
verbs, they can be divided into two types according to the subject: honorific language (尊敬語) and humble language (謙遜語).
A. Honorific language (respectful)
Honorific language is a way of expressing respect toward the agent of the action mentioned in the conversation. When using honorific
language, the subject should be the respected party.
Honorific verbs that express respect include:

行く・来る＝いらっしゃる/おいでになる
いる＝いらっしゃる

くれる＝くださる

become “いらっしゃいます”, “おっしゃいます”, “なさい” respectively when followed by ます.
ます”、“くださいます”。

honorific verb.

お + masu-stem of a native (wago) verb
+ する/いたす
ご + stem of a Sino-Japanese (kango) verb
Note: this pattern cannot be used when removing ます from the masu-form leaves only a single mora
き ね
