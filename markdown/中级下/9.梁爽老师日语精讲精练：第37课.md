Grammar Notes
1. The causative-passive form of verbs
Besides the causative and passive forms we learned earlier, Japanese verbs also have a
“causative-passive form” formed by combining the causative and the passive. To make the causative-passive, first change the verb to its caus-
ative form, and then change that into the passive. The specific rules are as follows:
Type I verbs: Type I verbs have two ways of conjugating.
First: change the final u-row kana of the verb to the corresponding a-row kana and attach “せられる”. Note that verbs ending in
“う” first change to “わ” before attaching “せられる”. For example:
か か
書く：かく→か＋せられる＝書かせられる
よ よ
読む：よむ→ま＋せられる＝読ませられる
つか つか
使う：つかう→わ＋せられる＝使わせられる
Second: change the final u-row kana of the verb to the corresponding a-row kana and attach “される”. This form is more col-
loquial.
か か
書く：かく→か＋される＝書かされる
よ よ
読む：よむ→ま＋される＝読まされる
つか つか
使う：つかう→わ＋される＝使わされる
はな なお だ け

are conjugated into the causative-passive using the first method above. For example:

2. Causative-passive sentences
Simply put, the causative-passive sentence is closer to an active sentence. For example:
わたし まんが す
① a. 私は漫画を捨てた。<active>
わたし まんが す
b. 私は漫画を捨てさせられた。<causative-passive>
わたし
In the examples above, the doer of the action in both a and b is “私”, and the action is the same: throwing away the manga.
Using the causative-passive form of the verb highlights the doer's psychological unwillingness and
displeasure when performing the action. The basic pattern of the causative-passive sentence is:
N は/が N に/から/によって N を V（さ）せられる
Here, N is the doer of the action, serving as subject marked with “は” or “が”; N is the
causer, i.e. the person or organization that makes N do something, usually marked with “に”; when emphasis on a
specific causer is needed, “から” or “によって” can also be used. But in causative-passive sentences, the causer
is generally omitted — example ①b omits it; N is the direct object of the action — in example ①b,
まんが ほん す
“漫画の本” is the direct object of the action “捨てる”, marked with “を”.

6. 〜あげく
Pattern: V た + あげく

The ta-form of a verb, or an action “noun + の”, followed by “あげく”, indicates that after going through the process
described in the first clause, the result described in the second clause is finally reached. The first clause often implies repeated attempts,
a long time spent, or great effort, while the second clause is mostly a regrettable result. Can be translated as “...,
and in the end ...”, “after ..., ended up ...”.
