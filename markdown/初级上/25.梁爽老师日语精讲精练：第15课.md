Grammar Notes
1. Japanese transitive/intransitive verbs and the particle “を” <“を”: marking the object>
Japanese verbs can be divided into intransitive and transitive verbs. Intransitive verbs cannot take an object, much like
“intransitive verbs” in English. Transitive verbs can take an object, much like the English “transitive verb” concept. Of
course, some individual Japanese verbs have both intransitive and transitive properties.
When using a transitive verb, the particle “を” is placed after the noun to mark the object of the verb.
ほん よ
① 本を読みます。／I read a book.
しりょう しら
② 資料を調べます。／I look up materials.
When the object part becomes the topic, the particle “は” or “も” can replace “を”.
おく てがみ か
③ メールはよく送りますが、手紙はあまり書きません。／I often send emails, but I don't write letters much.
やす ひ えいが み か もの
④ 休みの日は、よく映画を見ます。買い物もします。／On days off I often watch
movies. I also go shopping.
2. N で

Expression Expansion
1. About offering help
When you want to proactively offer help or do something for someone, you can use the following two ways to
express it.
(1) V ましょう／V ましょうか <volition, suggestion (V ましょう–2)>
In Lesson 14 we already learned the invitational use of “V ましょう”, i.e. suggesting that oth-
ers do something together with you. Besides invitation, “V ましょう” can also be used to proactively offer help
to someone, expressing the speaker's offer to perform an action themselves.
“か” carries a questioning tone; “V ましょうか” has the feel of seeking the other person's consent and is softer than
“V ましょう”. When using this pattern, the subject is mostly first person (usually omit-
ted), and the beneficiary of the action is mostly the listener.
てつだ
① A：手伝いましょうか。／Shall I help you?
ねが
B：すみません。お願いします。／Sorry to trouble you. Please do.
にもつ も
② A：荷物を持ちましょうか。／Shall I carry your luggage?
だいじょうぶ
B：大丈夫です。ありがとうございます。／I'm fine. Thank you.
じかん でんわ
③ メールでは時間がかかりますから、電話をかけましょうか。／Email
takes too long, so let me give them a call.
ぼく きょうりょく
（２）僕も協 力しますよ。
きょうりょく

rectly express your willingness to help. Note that when the first person does a favor for the second person,

2. How to respond when someone offers help
（１）to accept; to receive
When you are willing to accept the help offered, you can use the following sentences.
も
① A：それ、持ちましょうか。／Shall I carry that for you?
たす
B：助かります。ありがとうございます。／That's a great help. Thank you.
も
② A：それ、持ちましょうか。／Shall I carry that for you?
ねが
B：すみません。お願いします。／Sorry to trouble you. Please do.
たす
The “助かります。” in example ① means “to be helped; to be saved”. When someone offers
たす
to help, you can use “助かります。” to express gratitude.
ねが
The “お願いします” in example ② is a polite expression you can use when asking someone to do something for you,
translated as “please; I'm counting on you”.
（２）refusal; rejection
If you need to decline the other person's kind offer, you can first thank them and then use the following sentence.
も
① A：それ、持ちましょうか。／Shall I carry that for you?
だいじょうぶ
B：あ、大丈夫です。ありがとうございます。／Ah, I'm fine. Thank you.
3. Describing the sequence of actions or situations
あと さいご
You can place conjunctions such as “まず”, “それから”, “そして”, “その後”, and “最後に” at the
head of sentences to describe the sequence of actions or situations. These words mean “first”, “then”, “moreover”,
and “finally” respectively.
じぶん かんが
① まず、自分でテーマを考えます。／First, think of a topic yourself.
げんこう か
② それから、原稿を書きます。／Then, write the script.
じしょ たんご よ かた しら
③ そして、辞書で単語の読み方を調べます。／Also, look up the readings of words in a dictionary.
さいご れんしゅう
④ 最後に、たくさん練習します。／Finally, practice a lot.
