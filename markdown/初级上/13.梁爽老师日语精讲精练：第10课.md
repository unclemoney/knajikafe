Grammar Notes
1. Adjective-predicate sentences (polite) — past tense
Lesson 9 covered the non-past tense of adjectives (polite); here we learn the past tense of adjective-predicate sentences (polite).
The past-tense usage of adjective-predicate sentences (polite) requires different conjugations depending on the type of adjective,
as follows:
(1) Affirmative past form of adjective-predicate sentences (polite)
Type I adjectives: A い → かった + です。
Ⅰ
Type II adjectives: A + でした。
Ⅱ
(2) Negative past form of adjective-predicate sentences (polite)
Type I adjectives: A い → く + ありませんでした。
Ⅰ
Type II adjectives: A + ではありませんでした。
Ⅱ

2. The negative “ない”
“ない” means “not; there is no”, and is a Type I adjective. It is also an “auxiliary adjective” attached
after adjectives to express negation. “ない” followed by “です” is fairly polite and can be interchanged with “ありません”.
You can memorize the following “formula”:
ありません。＝ないです。
Then the past tense of the two is:
ありませんでした。＝なかったです。
Based on this formula, the polite affirmative and negative forms of adjective-predicate sentences in each tense (called the “adjective
four quadrants” in this book) can be summarized in Table 10-2.

3. The continuative form of adjectives <adjective ren'yokei>
The “continuative” in Japanese, also called “ren'yokei”, is “the form for connecting predicates”. The “continuative” is used at pause points
within a sentence, where the parts before and after the pause are mostly in a parallel, sequential relation. Sometimes we use two, three, or even more
adjectives to describe the qualities of the same thing. When these adjectives are in a parallel relation, the continuative
form of the adjective (i.e. its second ren'yokei) is needed. Different types of adjectives have different continuative forms, as follows:
Type I adjectives: A い → く + て, ...
Ⅰ
Type II adjectives: A + で, ...
Ⅱ
えいが しゅじんこう せ たか みりょくてき
① 映画の主人公は背が高くて、魅力的です。／The movie's protagonist is tall and attractive.
きのう しけんもんだい ふくざつ むずか
② 昨日の試験問題は複雑で、難しかったです。／Yesterday's exam questions were complicated and difficult.
Note: the continuative form is chosen according to the part of speech of the first item only, not determined by the second. For example:
えいが しゅじんこう せ たか
③−1 映画の主人公は背が高くて、かっこいいです。／The movie's protagonist is tall and cool.
えいが しゅじんこう せ たか みりょくてき
③−2 映画の主人公は背が高くて、魅力的です。／The movie's protagonist is tall and
very attractive.
せ たか
“背が高い” is a Type I adjective; whether it is followed by a Type I adjective (like “かっこいい” in ③-1)
みりょくてき たか せ
or a Type II adjective (like “魅力的” in ③-2) does not affect the continuative form of “高い”. That is, the continuative form of the first item “背が
たか
高い” is determined solely by its own part of speech and is unaffected by what follows.
In addition, tense is determined by the sentence-final predicate; the continuative part does not need to mark tense. For example:
せ たか みりょくてき
④ 昨日の映画の主人公は背が高くて、魅力的でした。／The protagonist of yesterday's movie was
tall and attractive.
4. N まで <end point>
The particle “まで” follows a noun and indicates an end point, equivalent to “until; up to; as far as”. “まで”
can follow a noun with temporal meaning or a time noun, as in this lesson, or a place noun to indicate the end point of a location. “まで” can also be followed directly by the copula “です” to end the sentence, forming a noun-predi-
cate sentence.

きのう おそ にほんご しけん
① わたしは昨日、遅くまで日本語の試験でした。／Yesterday my Japanese exam went until late.
きょう じゅぎょう
② 今日の授業はここまでです。／That's all for today's class.
かしゅ らいげつはつか
③ あの歌手のライブツアーは来月20日までです。／That singer's concert tour runs until the
20th of next month.

6. N とか ＜examples＞
The particle “とか” follows a noun and is used to give examples. “とか” is mostly used in spoken conversation; it can be used once in a sentence
or appear multiple times in the same sentence. When “とか” is used, the topic/subject particles “は”
and “が”, and the object particle “を”, can be omitted.
の もの す
① A: どんな飲み物が好きですか。／What drinks do you like?
B: ミルクティーとか、コーラとか。／Milk tea, cola, things like that.
もくよう び ご ご い こう
② 木曜日の午後以降とかはどうですか。／How about, say, Thursday afternoon or later?

2. About making suggestions
(1) Suggestion method 1: N はどうですか。<suggestion II>
Without using a verb, you can make a suggestion with the pattern “N はどうですか”. Here
N is the content of the suggestion, such as the time of an appointment, its content, or some recommended options. It means “How about ...?”.

(1) Agreeing

4. Common spoken responses in this lesson
（1）よかったですね。
“よかった” is the past form of the adjective “いい”. Adding
the exclamatory particle “ね” after “よかったです” expresses happiness about what happened to the other person;
it can be translated as “That's great”, “Good for you”.
せんしゅう しけん
① A：先週の試験、どうでしたか。／How was last week's exam?
まんてん
B：満点でしたよ。／I got full marks.
A：よかったですね！おめでとう。／That's great! Congratulations.
（2）まあ、なんとか。
The adverb “まあ” is mostly used in spoken language; it can stand alone or be used with a following predicate, expressing
basic agreement with what the other person said, translatable as “so-so”, “pretty good”, etc. This can be used
when someone asks you how things are going.
The adverb “なんとか” means “(though not as satisfying as hoped) somehow manageable”.
In spoken language, “なんとか” can be used alone or with a verb after it
(see Lesson 18 for details), with the meaning “to manage to ... somehow”, “finally ...”.
(3) “うん” and “ううん”
“うん” and “ううん” are the spoken forms of “はい” and “いいえ” respectively, used between people who are close,
to express agreement or denial. “うん” and “ううん” are generally not used toward superiors,
as it would sound impolite.
