9. V てあげる/くれる/もらう
In Lesson 18 we learned the verbs あげる/くれる/もらう for expressing the giving and receiving of things.
This lesson covers the use of these giving/receiving verbs after て as auxiliary verbs. Verbs
followed by てあげる/くれる/もらう express the giving and receiving of actions. The specific rules are as follows:

<!-- Page 9 -->

<Giving/receiving verb sentence I>
わたし
N (I / giver) は N (receiver) に V てあげる
<Giving/receiving verb sentence I> expresses doing something for someone else. When neither N nor N is "I",
only two relationships are possible between N and N:
・N and N are at the same psychological distance from the speaker.
・The speaker is psychologically closer to N.
Note, however, that the "V てあげる" pattern over-emphasizes that you deliberately did something for the other person,
which can make the listener feel they are being done a favor. So if the other person is not someone close to you,
avoid it as much as possible to prevent sounding rude (example ③). In such cases use "V ましょうか"
(example ④), which is more polite.
かんこうきゃく みち おし
① 観光客に道を教えてあげます。／(I'll) give directions to the tourists.
ゆ か お い ちゅう か りょう り つく

せんせい おも も

わたし

<Giving/receiving verb sentence III> expresses receiving someone's doing something for you. The subject of this pattern
わたし
is the receiver of the action, i.e., "I", and 私は is often omitted. The giver of the action is usually marked with に
or から. Both "V てくれる" and "V てもらう" can express that the speaker receives someone's doing some-

toward the giver of the action.
