Grammar Notes
1. 〜と、〜 <condition>
Pattern: N だ + と、〜
Aい+と、〜
Ⅰ
A だ+と、〜
Ⅱ
V る+と、〜
The dictionary form of a verb or i-adjective, or "noun/na-adjective + だ", followed by "と" forms a
conditional sentence expressing that whenever the former holds, the latter invariably follows.
Translate as "when... (then)...", "if..., ...". This pattern is also often used when giving directions, as in ④.
い へ や あか
① スイッチを入れると、部屋が明るくなります。／When you flip the switch, the room
lights up.
びんぼう しゅうしょく けっこん
② 貧乏だと、就 職も結婚もできない。／If you're poor, you can neither find a job
nor get married.
きおん ひく さくら さ
③ 気温が低いと、桜が咲きません。／If the temperature is low, the cherry blossoms won't bloom.
い ひだり て おお ちゅうしゃじょう み
④ まっすぐ行くと、左 手に大きな駐車場が見えます。／Go straight, and on your left
you'll see a large parking lot.
2. V ておく
The te-form of a verb followed by "ておく" expresses doing something in advance for a certain need,
or keeping a certain state as a temporary measure — "...in advance; ...and leave it (as is)". In speech,
"V ておく" often contracts to "V とく".
あしたで くるま じゅうでん
① 明日出かけるので、車を充電しておいてください。／Because I'm going out tomorrow,
Before leaving, please charge the car in advance.

5. 〜のに ＜contrast＞
Pattern: N な + のに
V/A attributive form + のに
"Noun + な" or the attributive form of a verb/adjective followed by the particle "のに" forms an adversative
compound sentence: the premise or cause in the first clause does not lead to the expected result in the second.
The speaker often sounds surprised or dissatisfied. Translate as "...yet...; although...". Both clauses of "のに"
are usually established facts; in particular, the second clause cannot be a command, request, or wish.
しがつ なつ あつ
① まだ4月なのに、夏よりも暑い。／It's only April, yet it's hotter than summer.
きょう おおゆき ふ あさ じゅぎょう
② 今日は大雪が降っているのに、朝から授業がある。／It's snowing heavily today, yet
we have classes from the morning.
くすり の たいちょう よ
③ もう薬を飲んでいるのに、体調はぜんぜん良くならない。／I'm already taking medicine,
yet I'm not getting any better.
6. 〜らしい <hearsay>
"らしい" attaches to the plain form of a verb/adjective (na-adjectives drop だ) or a noun, expressing hearsay
or conjecture based on objective grounds — "it seems...; I hear...; apparently...". The objective source can be
き
marked with "〜によると" or "聞いたところでは".
き はなし たなか らいげつしごと や
① 聞いた 話 では、田中さんは来月仕事を辞めるらしいです。／From what I hear,
Tanaka is apparently quitting his job next month.
かいちょう きょうけっせき れんらく はい
② 会長は今日欠席するらしいですよ。さっき連絡が入りました。／It seems the chairman
will be absent today. A notice just came in.

③ あの人は業界ではかなり有名らしい。／That person seems quite famous in the industry.

11. 〜なんて
"なんて" can follow certain words or plain sentences, presenting the content before "なんて" as
presents it as a topic. The content before "なんて" is usually something the listener already knows; the predicate
after "なんて" is usually the speaker's evaluation of it, often with surprise, strong negation, or contempt.
"なんて" is mostly used in spoken language.
たから あ
① 宝くじに当たったなんて、すごいですね。／(To think you) won the lottery — amazing!

やさい きら
② 野菜なんて、嫌い！／Vegetables? I hate them!
ひと かちょう しん
③ あんな人が課長だなんて、信じられない！／(To think) someone like that is a section chief —
I can't believe it!
