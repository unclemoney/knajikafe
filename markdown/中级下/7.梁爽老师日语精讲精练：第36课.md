Grammar Notes
1. わけではない ＜partial negation＞
The attributive form of a verb or adjective followed by “わけではない/わけじゃない” is used to deny the inevitable conclusion drawn from the situation described in the current or
preceding text, emphasizing that this is not the case. Can be translated as “it is not
that ...”, “not necessarily ...”.
りょうり りょうり ひま
① 料理ができないわけではない。料理をする暇がないだけだ。／It's not that I can't cook. I just don't have time to cook.
しょさい ほん なら ぜんぶ よ
② 書斎にたくさんの本が並べてあるが、全部読んだわけではない。／Many books are lined up in the
study, but I haven't read them all.
しごと しめい ひ う
③ その仕事をやりたいわけではないけど、指名されたから引き受けたの
だ。／It's not that I wanted to do that job; I took it on only because I was named.

8. 〜の？
In plain-form conversation, the sentence-final particle “の” expresses a question and can be used by both men and women. In this
case, use rising intonation.
