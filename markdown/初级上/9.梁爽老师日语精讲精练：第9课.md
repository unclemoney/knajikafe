Grammar Notes
1. Adjective predicate sentences
Adjectives as predicates express states or constant properties. In Japanese, adjectives are divided into two types: type I
adjectives (ending in い) and type II adjectives (in Japanese "school grammar" type II adjectives are called "adjec-
tive verbs"). The polite non-past forms of adjective predicate sentences differ according to the type:
(1) Polite non-past (present or future) affirmative forms of adjective predicate sentences
Type I adjective: A ＋ です。
Ⅰ
Type II adjective: A ＋ です。
Ⅱ
(2) Polite non-past (present or future) negative forms of adjective predicate sentences
Type I adjective: A い→く＋ありません。
Ⅰ
Type II adjective: A ＋では（じゃ）ありません。
Ⅱ

2. N は N が〜。
This is a typical expression favored by Japanese speakers, unlike what Chinese speakers are used to. The particle
は marks the topic of the sentence (also called the "big subject"), and が marks the subject (the "small subject"). In the pattern "A は
B が X です", the statement is mainly about the topic A (A is usually the whole of a person or thing),
B is usually a part of A, and "B が X です" expresses A's characteristic. For example:
A B X

The degree adverbs あまり and そんなに can both be paired with the negative form of adjectives,
equivalent to "not very ...", "not that ..." in English.

The interrogative どう means "how". "N は どうですか" is used to ask about

6. Sentence + よ。 <assertion; calling attention>
The particle よ is used at the end of a sentence to express the speaker's assertion or declaration, an emphatic nuance.

③ 日本語はそんなに難しくありませんよ。／Japanese isn't that hard, you know.
7. Sentence + ね。 <mild exclamation (particle ね II)>
When the particle ね at the end of a sentence expresses mild exclamation, it is read with falling intonation; depending on the nuance, it can also be lengthened
to ねえ.

③ 由加ちゃんのこのスマホケース、かわいいですね。（↘）／Yuka, this phone case of yours
is so cute.

3. Common spoken expressions in this lesson
なかむら
（１）中村さんのおかげですよ。
This is a very polite expression meaning "it's all thanks to Nakamura". You can use "〜
さんのおかげです" to express gratitude to someone.
せんせい ごうかく
① 先生：合格、おめでとう。／Congratulations on passing the exam!
がくせい せんせい
学生：ありがとうございます。先生のおかげです。／Thank you. It's all thanks to you,
teacher.
（２）どういたしまして。
This is the reply to "ありがとうございます", meaning "you're welcome". "どういたしま
して" sounds rather formal. In a relaxed atmosphere, you can reply to thanks with "いいえ",
which sounds more natural.
ゆ か なかむら
① 由加：中村さんのおかげですよ。いろいろありがとう。／It's all thanks to you, Nakamura. Thank you for everything.
なかむら
中村：どういたしまして。／You're welcome.
② A：ありがとう。／Thanks.
B：いいえ。／Not at all.

（３）どうしてですか。
The interrogative どうして means "why". It can be followed by ですか to directly ask for a reason.
In this lesson, Yuka guesses whether Nakamura might be a Capricorn, and Nakamura wonders why Yuka asks that,
so she asks "どうしてですか。" in Japanese, meaning "why do you ask?".
（４）いえいえ。
"いえいえ" is also a colloquial form of "いいえ", with a stronger tone than "いえ", mostly used
to humbly respond to someone's compliment.
① A：Bさんは本当に性格がいいですね。／You really have a nice personality.
B：いえいえ、そんなことはありませんよ。／No, no, that's not true.
たんじょうび
（５）誕生日パーティーをしましょう。
It means "let's have a birthday party". "〜しましょう" means "let's (do) ... together";
this grammar will be learned in Lesson 14.
あと ちゃ
① A：この後、お茶をしましょう。／Let's have tea later.
B：いいですね。／Sounds good.
