3. Interrogative + が <marking the subject>
The case particle が follows a noun to mark the "subject". When an interrogative word is the subject,
it must be marked with が, and the answer must also use が. When an interrogative is the subject, the particle
は cannot be used.

4. Japanese "pre-noun adjectivals" (rentaishi)
"この" "その" "あの" "どの" are "pre-noun adjectivals" and cannot be used independently. They can only precede a
noun, indicating and qualifying the person or thing referred to; they belong to the ko-so-a-do word family.
おとうと だいがく
① 弟 さんはどの大学にいますか。／Which university is your brother at?
わたし へ や たてもの にかい
② 私の部屋はあの建物の2階です。／My room is on the 2nd floor of that building.
"Pre-noun adjectival" is one of the Japanese parts of speech. As the name suggests, it is "a word that connects to a substantive".
That is, a substantive (i.e., a noun) must follow it. For example:
The Japanese for "This is my computer" should be:
わたし
③-1. （○）これは私のパソコンです。／This is my computer.
わたし
③-2. （×）このは私のパソコンです。
In ③-2, "この" is a pre-noun adjectival, so a substantive must follow it, not the particle は. As for "this
computer is mine", the Japanese is:
わたし
④-1. （○）このパソコンは私のです。／This computer is mine.
わたし
④-2. （×）これパソコンは私のです。
"This computer" must be formed with the pre-noun adjectival この followed by パソコン, while in ④-2 "こ
れ" and "パソコン" are both substantives and cannot be connected.
