Grammar Notes
1. Adjectives modifying nouns <attributive form of adjectives>
Besides serving as predicates, adjectives can also be placed before nouns as modifiers — called the “attributive form
of the adjective” in Japanese grammar. As follows:
Type I adjectives: A + noun
Ⅰ
Type II adjectives: A + な + noun
Ⅱ
Type I adjectives can directly precede the noun they modify. Type II adjectives, when modifying a noun, need to be fol-
lowed by “な” before the noun. For this reason, some grammar books also call Type II adjectives “na-adjectives”, and
Type I adjectives ending in “い” “i-adjectives”.
しろ
① 白いパソコン／a white computer
まち
② きれいな町／a clean, pretty town
2. N が <subject>

rules for using “は” and “が” mentioned earlier (Lesson 7, Grammar 3 and Lesson 9, Grammar 2),
within a stretch of context, when the subject is unknown (i.e. new to the listener) information, it must be marked with “が”.
Known, old information is marked with “は”. Compare:

7. Japanese “comparison” patterns
There are many ways to express comparison in Japanese. Here we summarize the comparison patterns into the fol-
lowing forms:
（１）句型1 N は N より〜。
“より” is a case particle indicating the standard of comparison. This pattern says that N has more of the characteristic
shown by the predicate than N. Japanese “A はBより〜” is generally translated as “A is more ... than B”. When A has much more of the quality
explained in the predicate than B, Japanese often uses the adverb “ずっと”, translated as “much ...”.

ちゅうごく にほん ひろ
① 中国は日本より広いです。／China is bigger than Japan.
おお
② （こちらのパソコンは）さっきのよりメモリーがずっと大きいですよ。
／(This computer) has much more memory than the one from before.
ねだん たか
③ スマホはパソコンより値段が高いです。／Smartphones are more expensive than computers.

（２）句型2 N より N のほうが〜。
This pattern is a variant of Pattern 1, meaning that compared with N, N has more of the characteristic
ほう
shown by the predicate. “ほう” can also be written “方”; it is a noun meaning “a certain side/direction”. “BよりA
のほうが〜” can be translated as “A is more ... than B”. Likewise, this pattern can also serve as the answer to Pattern 4
questions.
にほん ちゅうごく ひろ
① 日本より中国のほうが広いです。／Compared with Japan, China is bigger.
ねだん たか
② パソコンよりスマホのほうが値段が高いです。／Compared with computers, smartphones are
more expensive.
（３）句型3 N は N ほど〜（否定）。
The adverbial particle “ほど” follows a noun and indicates degree. When followed by a negative, this pattern says N does not have
the characteristic to the same degree as N. “Bは A ほど〜 (negative)” can be translated as “B is not as ... as A”,
“B doesn't ... as much as A”.
にほん ちゅうごく ひろ
① 日本は中国ほど広くありません。／Japan is not as big as China.
ねだん たか
② パソコンはスマホほど値段が高くありません。／Computers are not as expensive
as smartphones.
（４）句型4 N と N とどちら（のほう）が〜か。
This pattern is the question form of comparison sentences. It asks which of N and N has more of the characteristic
shown by the predicate. “A と B とどちら（のほう）が〜か” can be translated as “Which is (more) ..., A or B?”.
When choosing between the two, the answer usually uses the Pattern 2 form, with the “B より” part mostly omitted —
just answer “A のほうが〜”.

2. Polite spoken expressions in this lesson
In many situations, Japanese people need to use
ていねい ご
“polite language” (Japanese: “丁寧語”, often translated “courteous style”) when speaking to customers or people of higher status. This lesson covers several spoken expressions that become “polite language” just by swapping
specific words.
（１）では、こちらはいかがですか。
It means “Well then, how about this one?”. The conjunction “では” at the head of a sentence can be translated as “well then”,
and is often shortened to “じゃ” in spoken language.
“こちら” is the polite form of “これ”. “こちら” can also serve as the polite form of
“ここ”, referring to the speaker's location or things near the speaker. This substitution also applies to the other “こそ
あど” words.
“いかが” is the polite form of “どう”, also meaning “how; what about”. “こちらは
いかがですか” means basically the same as “これはどうですか”, but the former is more formal and
polite.
やす
① A：もっと安いパソコンはありませんか。／Do you have a cheaper computer?
B：はい、こちらです。／Yes, this one.
らいしゅう
②−1 来週はどうですか。／How about next week?
