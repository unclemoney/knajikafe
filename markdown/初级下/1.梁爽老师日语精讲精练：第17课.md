Grammar Notes
1. Plain forms of verbs
Lesson 13 covered the plain forms of nouns and adjectives. Like noun sentences and adjective sentences, verb-final
verb sentences also have corresponding polite and plain forms.
Among them, "Vる (verb dictionary form)", and "Vない" and "Vなかっ
た" related to the verb imperfective form will be detailed in Grammar 2 and 3 of this lesson. Here the polite and plain forms of verbs are summarized in
Table 17.
Table 17-1: Four-quadrant comparison of polite and plain verb sentences
Affirmative  Negative
Present  Polite: V ます。  Polite: Vません。
Future  Plain: Vる (verb dictionary form)  Plain: Vない。
Polite: V ました。  Polite: Vませんでした。
Past
Plain: V た。  Plain: Vなかった。
Polite style is mostly used in formal settings; its tone is formal and courteous, so it is also called "polite style" — in Japanese,
ていねいご
"丁寧語". In spoken settings, plain style sounds more casual and intimate and is mostly used in conversations among friends or family;
in written settings, plain style emphasizes a more argumentative or declarative tone, so in writing it
is mostly used for papers, news reports, and the like.
2. V る (verb dictionary form)
The affirmative plain form in the Japanese non-past tense is the verb's "dictionary form", also called the "verb original
form" or "verb basic form". This is the most basic form of the verb; since dictionary entries appear in this
form, it is usually called the "dictionary form". For example:
えつらん
① 敬体：スマホでウェブサイトを閲覧します。／Browse websites on a phone.
えつらん
Plain form: スマホでウェブサイトを閲覧する。／Browse websites on a phone.
てつや
② 敬体：よく徹夜します。／Often stay up all night.
てつや
Plain form: よく徹夜する。／Often stay up all night.

3. V ない (verb imperfective/nai-form)
The negative plain form of a verb is formed by attaching the negative auxiliary ない to the verb.
Generally called the "nai-form" or "negative form" of the verb, also the "imperfective form".
The rules for attaching ない to verbs are as follows:
Group I (godan) verbs:
Change the final kana of the verb from the u-row to the a-row, then add ない. Note that
for type I verbs ending in う, change the final う to わ before adding ない.
For example:
つか つか
使う：つかう→わ＋ない＝使わない
ある ある
歩く：あるく→か＋ない＝歩かない
かえ かえ
帰る：かえる→ら＋ない＝帰らない
Group II (ichidan) verbs:
Remove the final kana る, then add ない. For example:
み み
見る：みる＋ない＝見ない
た た
食べる：たべる＋ない＝食べない
あつ あつ
集める：あつめる＋ない＝集めない
Group III (irregular) verbs:
① Suru-verb: する. Change する to し, then add ない. That is:
する：する→し＋ない＝しない
りよう りよう りよう
利用する：利用する→し＋ない＝利用しない
く く こ
② Kuru-verb: 来る. Change 来る to 来, then add ない. That is:
く く こ
来る：来る→こ＋ない＝来ない
Note that the negative form of ある is not あらない, but the adjective な
い”。

5. N (period) に N (quantity) V <frequency>
The particle に follows a noun expressing a period (usually a time span), indicating the frequency or quantity
of an action within that period. Equivalent to "per ..." in English.
