1. Learn the plain forms of noun sentences and adjective-predicate sentences.

Grammar Notes
1. N (place) + へ（／に）+ movement V <movement>
The particle “へ” follows a noun indicating a place and marks the direction of movement, used together with movement predi-
い く かえ
cate verbs (such as “行く”, “来る”, “帰る”). In this case “へ” is not read “he”
but “e”. The particle “へ” can also be replaced by “に”.
い
① 毎朝、会社へ行きます。／I go to the company every morning.
かえ
② いつ帰りますか。／When are you coming back?
きょねん かぞく おおさか き
③ 去年、家族と大阪に来ました。／I came to Osaka with my family last year.
2. Question word + も <total negation>
A question word followed by the particle “も” and used with a negative predicate expresses total negation,
equivalent to “no ... at all”, “not any ...”.
よなか はくぶつかん だれ
① 夜中の博物館には誰もいません。／There is no one in the museum late at night.
ひ だ なに
② 引き出しには何もありません。／There is nothing in the drawer.
りょう り
③ ここのカレー 料 理はどれもおいしくないです。／None of the curry dishes here are
any good.
しゅうまつ い
④ この週末はどこにも行きません。／I'm not going anywhere this weekend.
3. Question word + か <indefinite>
A question word followed by the particle “か” expresses indefiniteness. This usage is not limited to questions; it
can also be used in declarative sentences. Note that when “question word + か” is used in a question, it
forms a yes-no question, and the answer usually starts with “はい” or “いいえ”.
きゃく なんにん
① あのレストランにお 客 さんが何人かいます。／There are a few customers
in that restaurant.
とく じゅうよう りゆう
② 特に重要な理由がいくつかあります。／There are several particularly important reasons.
だれ
③ A：あのう、すみません。誰かいますか。／Excuse me, is anyone there?
B：はい。／Yes.
いぜん なんかい き
④ 以前、何回か来ました。／I've been here a few times before.

4. N から <starting point>
The particle “から” can follow nouns indicating time, place, or order, marking the starting point of time, place,
or sequence — like “from ...” in English. “から” can also be followed directly by the copula
“です” to form a noun-predicate sentence.
からだ あした
① 体のトレーニングは明日からです。／Physical training starts tomorrow.
いま せいせき はっぴょう
② 今から成績の発表です。／The announcement of grades starts now.
ないよう
③ ここからはテストの内容です。／From here on is the content of the test.
The starting-point “から” can also be paired with the end-point particle “まで”.
かいぎ こんげつ よっか むいか
④ 会議は今月の4日から6日までです。／The meeting is from the 4th to the 6th of this month.
5. Ｎじゃありませんか。
This pattern is the rhetorical-question form of the noun-predicate sentence “Nです”. Equivalent to “isn't it ...?
”, with a tone of surprise or exclamation; mostly used in spoken language.
ゆうめい けんきゅうじょ
① とても有名な研究所じゃありませんか。／Isn't this a really famous research
institute!
じゅうにじごじっぷん
② もう 1 2時50分じゃありませんか。／It's already 12:50!
ひと すずき
③ あ、あの人は、鈴木さんじゃありませんか。／Ah, isn't that Suzuki over there?
6. けど <softened contrast, indirect tone>
The conjunctive particle “けど” is the shortened form of “けれども” and expresses contrast. In this lesson, “けど”
is used at the end of a sentence for a softened contrast. This expression trails off, omitting the second half of the sentence; the tone is fairly
indirect and is mostly used in spoken language. Besides “けど”, “けれども” also has the forms “けれど” and “けど
も”.
かいぎ
① これから会議なんですけど。／We're about to have a meeting...
じかん
② そろそろお時間ですけれども。／It's about time...
なかむら
③ A：中村さんはいますか。／Is Nakamura here?
わたし
B：はい、私ですけど。／Yes, that's me.

1. The plain forms of nouns and adjectives, and their use in plain-style conversation
(1) Plain forms of noun-predicate sentences and adjective-predicate sentences
Japanese mainly has two speech styles: “polite” and “plain”. The plain style, as opposed to the polite style, is mostly used in conversations between friends
or family, or in written forms such as novels and papers. When using Japanese, you need to choose the appropriate
style according to the relationship between the two parties.

(2) Switching between polite and plain styles in speech
The speech style in spoken Japanese changes according to the listener, the situation, and the topic. One such situa-
tion is that even in a mainly polite-style conversation, the plain style can be used when the speaker voices their own feelings (such as surprise or emer-
かいわ ほんとう
gency). The “本当だ！”, “やばい！”
としょかん
and “じゃ、図書館だ！” in this lesson's model dialogue are Nakamura's self-talk when feeling surprised or flustered, and therefore
use the plain form.
や
① このたこ焼き、おいしいね。／This takoyaki is tasty.
か じ
② 火事だ！／Fire!
くん
③ ハチ君、かわいい！／Hachi is so cute!

2. Other common spoken expressions in this lesson
か
（１）お借りします。
When borrowing something from someone, Japanese people generally use this phrase as a courtesy. It is a polite expression,
meaning “let me borrow this”.
じてんしゃ か
① A：そこの自転車、お借りしますね。／That bicycle — I'll borrow it, okay?
B：はーい。／Sure.
い
（２）行ってきます。
Japanese people have set greetings for leaving and returning. When about to leave (home, the office, etc.),
い
they say “行ってきます。”, meaning “I'm off”, “I'll be back”. The people staying behind
い
reply “行ってらっしゃい”, equivalent to “take care; see you later”.
In addition, upon returning they say “ただいま。 (I'm home)”, and the people at home (or in the office)
かえ
respond with “お帰りなさい。” (welcome back).
むすこ い
① (about to leave) 息子：行ってきます。／I'm off.
はは い
母：行ってらっしゃい。／See you later.
むすこ
② (coming back in) 息子：ただいま。／I'm home.
はは かえ
母：お帰りなさい。／Welcome home.
