Grammar Notes
1. N を <marking the object>
The particle を follows a noun to mark the object. Japanese is an SOV (subject-object-verb)
language, where the predicate verb usually comes at the end of the sentence and the object comes earlier, marked with
を.
"ください" is the imperative form of the verb くださる (to give), equivalent to "please give me
...". "Nをください。" is mostly used when shopping or ordering food.
すいぞくかん にゅうじょうけん
① 水族館の入 場券をください。／I'd like an admission ticket to the aquarium, please.
② チーズバーガーセットをください。／I'd like a cheeseburger set, please.
③ ビールをください。／A beer, please.
To express "I also want ...", use the particle も meaning "also". も and を
cannot be used together; も must replace を.
④ それから、ホットコーヒーもください。／And also, a hot coffee please.
2. N を (quantity) ください。
To express the quantity you want to buy, place the quantifier before the verb ください. For
ひょうげん ひろ
quantifier expressions, see this lesson's "Expanding Expressions" 1-(2).

① A：この水族館の入 場 券を3枚ください。／Three admission tickets to the aquarium, please.
さんぜん えん
B：はい、3000円です。／Certainly, that will be 3,000 yen.

4. N でございます。
The assertive auxiliary でございます is the formal version of です. Formal style is mostly
used on formal occasions such as speeches or international conferences, or by service-industry staff toward customers. When used,
the subject is generally oneself or one's own side's people or things; it generally cannot be used for the respected listener
or their people or things.
① エレベーターはこちらでございます。／The elevator is this way.
わたし たんとうしゃ

5. どうなさいますか。
The interrogative どう is equivalent to "how". なさいます is the honorific verb for する
(to do); using なさいます makes the speaker's tone more polite and formal.
"どうなさいますか" is equivalent to "How would you like it?" "What would you like to do?"
mostly used by service-industry staff. For example:
① A：ホットコーヒーのサイズはどうなさいますか。／What size would you like for your hot coffee?
エル ねが
B：Lサイズでお願いします。／A large, please.
When the interrogative どう serves as the predicate, it can be followed by ですか to form the wh-question "〜はどうで
すか。", used to ask the listener's opinion about the thing the subject refers to. For example:
② このコーヒーはどうですか。／How is this coffee?
せいかだいがく
③ 清華大学はどうですか。／How is Tsinghua University?
さいきん
④ 最近はどうですか。／How have you been lately?

2. Common expressions for shopping, ordering, and paying
(1) Step 1: State your needs
When ordering at a restaurant or shopping at a store, you can use "Nをください。" to say "I'd like ..."
ねが
"please give me ..." to state your needs. You can also use the pattern "Nをお願いします。", which
is more polite and courteous.
① ビールをください。／A beer, please.
ねが
② ヨーグルトをお願いします。／Yogurt, please.
(2) Step 2: State the means or method of fulfilling your needs
After stating your needs, if you want to add the means by which you want them fulfilled, you can use "Nで
お願いします。".
エス ねが
① コーヒーはSサイズでお願いします。／For the coffee, a small size please.
② （when asking the clerk to wrap a gift）リボンはゴールドでお願いします。／For the rib-
bon, gold please.

3. Common replies in this lesson to requests or instructions from others
（１）わかりました。
To respond positively to a request or instruction, you can use "わかりました。" or "はい、わ
かりました。" — meaning "Understood", "Got it".
やす
① A：ゆっくり休んでくださいね。／Please get some good rest.
B：わかりました。／Got it.
ねが
② A：明日もお願いします。／I'm counting on you tomorrow too.
B：はい、わかりました。／Yes, understood.
（２）かしこまりました。
This is a set phrase, translatable as "Understood", "Certainly". It is mostly used by subordinates giving a positive reply
to a superior's instruction. It is more formal and polite than "わかりました。", and is mostly used by service-industry staff.
