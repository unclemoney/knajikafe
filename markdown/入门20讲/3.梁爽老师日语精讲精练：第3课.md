Japanese pronunciation rules (2): voiced sounds / semi-voiced sounds
Adding the voiced-sound mark 【゛】 to the upper right of the “か-row”, “さ-row”, “た-row”, and “は-row” kana in the gojuon chart forms the voiced
sounds. Adding the semi-voiced mark 【○】 to the upper right of the “は-row” kana forms the
semi-voiced sounds.

Contracted sounds in loanwords
Besides the contracted sounds learned above, Japanese loanwords sometimes use special contracted sounds
to approximate the original pronunciation in the source language. Let's try reading these Japanese loanwords.
Example words
1 ファン（fan）① 〔名〕 fan; enthusiast
2 アイディア（idea）①③ 〔名〕 idea
3 ウェブサイト（web site）③ 〔名〕 website

Common greetings (3)
1. ただいま。④ I'm home. / I'm back.
This is a commonly used Japanese greeting, said when you open the door and enter your own home.
かえ
2. お帰りなさい。⑥ Welcome home.
This phrase is used in response to “ただいま”. The person coming home says “ただいま” at the door,
かえ
and the people at home reply “お帰りなさい”.
げんき げんき
3. お元気ですか。②（“元気”①） How are you?
げん き げんき
“元気” means being healthy and energetic. Adding the prefix “お” before “元気” makes it
polite. The sentence-final particle “か” is a question particle used in questions; in this case the intonation
at the end generally rises.
げんき
4. おかげさまで、元気です。⓪＋① Thanks to you, I'm very well.
“おかげさまで” means “thanks to you”.
