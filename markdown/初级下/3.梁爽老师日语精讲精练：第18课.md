Grammar Notes
1. あげる/くれる/もらう <Giving/receiving verbs>
Unlike Chinese, Japanese uses different words for "give" depending on the subject. The most
representative are "あげる", "くれる", and "もらう". This set of verbs expresses the giving and
receiving of things, and they are called "giving/receiving verbs". The specific rules are as follows:
Ⅰ. N (giver/I) は N (receiver) に N (thing) をあげる
"あげる" means "to give". It is used when "I" give something to someone else. When neither N is
"me", only two relationships are possible between the two Ns:
・Both Ns are at the same psychological distance from the speaker (as in example ②).
・The speaker feels psychologically closer to one N (as in example ③).
かのじょ はな
① （わたしは）彼女に花をあげました。／I gave my girlfriend flowers.
にほん じょせい だんせい
② 日本では、バレンタインデーには女性が男性にチョコレートをあげ
ます。／In Japan, girls give boys chocolate on Valentine's Day.
いもうと ともだち みや げ
③ 妹 が友達にお土産をあげました。／My younger sister gave her friend a souvenir.
Besides "あげる", "やる" also means "to give", and its usage is basically
the same as "あげる". "やる" tends to emphasize giving to people of lower status than the speaker (such as one's
younger siblings) or to things (such as flowers, birds, pets). However, it is rarely used in modern Japanese.
はな みず
④ 花に水をやります。／Water the flowers.
Ⅱ. N (giver) は N (receiver/me) に N をくれる
"くれる" also means "to give". But unlike "あげる", "くれる" refers to
someone else giving something to "me" (as in example ⑤). Therefore, the subject must always be "someone else". When
neither N is "me", only one relationship is possible: compared with one N, the speak-
er feels psychologically closer to the other N (as in example ⑥).

In Japanese, different giving/receiving verbs must be used according to person restrictions, and this
is one reason why Japanese often omits the subject. In fact, in Japanese, from the predicate verb alone, together with the
surrounding context, the listener can easily tell who the subject of the sentence refers to.

4. ずつ
The particle "ずつ" follows a quantity word or degree adverb, and expresses doing something repeatedly in fixed amounts,
or distributing equally in the same amount or proportion.

① 1人ずつ発言する。／Speak one by one.

② 日本語が少しずつ上手になりました。／My Japanese has improved little by little.
ひとりさんこ
③ みかんを一人3個ずつあげます。／Give each person 3 mandarin oranges.
5. N にする <Choice>
A noun followed by "にする" expresses choosing one thing from among many options. Equivalent to "choose..."
or "decide on...". Often used when ordering food, shopping, etc.

いろ いっしょく

6. Adjective continuative form
Pattern: i-adjective A い→く + V
Ⅰ
na-adjective A + に + V
Ⅱ
The "adjective continuative form" is the form in which an adjective is followed by an inflecting word (i.e., a verb). With its continuative
form, an adjective can modify a verb, describing the manner in which the action proceeds or how it is carried out. In other words, this
is the adverbial use of adjectives. When an i-adjective is followed by a verb, its final "い" changes to "く"
before the verb; when a na-adjective is followed by a verb, "に" is added to its stem before the verb.
みな しつもん しんせつ こた
① 皆さんからの質問に親切に答えます。／I answer everyone's questions kind-
ly.
さら あら
② お皿をきれいに洗いました。／I washed the dishes clean.

Expression Expansion
1. とんでもないです。
The original meaning of "とんでもない" is "completely unexpected, unreasonable". From this it extends to
negating the other person's statement; it can be translated as "not at all" or "don't mention it".
たんじょう び
2. お誕 生 日、おめでとうございます。
“おめでとうございます” means “congratulations.” “ございます” is used to express
politeness and can be left untranslated in Chinese. Between very close acquaintances, “ございます” may also be
omitted. Before “おめでとうございます”, you can also add the occasion being congratulated, such as “お
たんじょうび けっこん にゅうしゃ しゅうしょく
誕生日” (birthday), “ご結婚” (marriage), “ご入社” (joining a company), “ご就 職” (starting a job), etc.
3. よかった。
Means “what a relief / thank goodness.” It is mostly an inner exclamation
of joy when something the speaker was worried about did not happen.
