Grammar Notes
1. -ba form <conditional form of verbs and adjectives>
Both verbs and adjectives can take the -ba conditional form (also called the provisional form), meaning "if..."
.
The rules for forming the -ba form of verbs are as follows:
Group I (godan) verbs:
Change the final u-dan kana of the verb to the corresponding e-dan kana, then add "ば". For example:
やす
休む：やすむ→め＋ば＝休めば
い い
行く：いく→け＋ば＝行けば
Group II (ichidan) verbs:
Change the final kana "る" of the verb to "れ", then add "ば". For example:
しら しら
調べる：しらべる→れ＋ば＝調べれば
Group III (irregular) verbs:
① Suru-verbs: change the final "る" to "れ", then add "ば". That is:
する：する→れ＋ば＝すれば
べんきょう

② Kuru-verb: change the final "る" to "れ", then add "ば". That is:

4. V てある <resulting state of an object>
A transitive verb followed by "てある" mainly expresses that after someone has deliberately done something,
the resulting state continues to exist. It can be translated as "(something) has been ...-ed (and remains so)".
With "V てある", the verb is usually a volitional transitive verb, and its object is marked with "が" instead.
かべ は
a. 壁にポスターが貼ってあります。／There is a poster (put up) on the wall.
かべ は
b. ポスターを壁に貼りました。／(Someone) put the poster up on the wall.
Compared with sentence b, sentence a emphasizes the state in which the poster remains on the wall
after having been put up.

5. V るように＜purpose＞
Pattern: V る + ように、〜
V ない+ように、〜
The dictionary form or nai-form of a verb followed by "ように" forms a purpose clause, expressing that one does
(or doesn't do) something so that a certain state comes about; it can be translated as "so that...".
The main clause that follows generally expresses the means or action taken to achieve that purpose.
Note that the verb before "ように" is usually a non-volitional verb such as "なる" or "できる",
or a weakly volitional form such as the potential or negative form.

Expression Notes
そういえば
"そういえば" is used when something comes to mind mid-conversation, or to change the topic.
It is often used to bring up a shared topic one has just recalled or something one has experienced.
It can be translated as "come to think of it..." or "now that you mention it...".
ふ じさん のぼ い
① A：そういえば、いつか富士山に登りたいと言っていましたよね。
／Come to think of it, you said you wanted to climb Mt. Fuji sometime.
B：うん。／Yeah.
し
② A：そういえば、知ってる？／Hey, come to think of it, did you know?
B：なに？／What?
