Grammar Notes
1. N ではなく（て）
When a noun is followed by "ではなく（て）", it negates the content preceding "ではなく（て）" and
adds the correct content after it. It can be translated as "not..., but...". "ではなく" is mostly used
in written form, while "ではなくて" or "じゃなくて" is mostly used in spoken language.
まえ た りょうり わしょく かんこくりょうり
① この前いっしょに食べた 料理は和食 じゃなくて、韓国料理だっ
たんだよ。／What we ate together the other day wasn't Japanese food, it was Korean food.
まえ やまだ き ひと やまだ こいびと
② この前、山田さんといっしょに来た人は、山田さんの恋人ではなく
いもうと
て、 妹 さんでした。／The person who came with Yamada the other day wasn't his girlfriend, but his
younger sister.
べんとう にほんとくゆう ぶんか せかいじゅう
③ 弁当は日本特有の文化ではなく、世界中どこにでもある。／Bento is not a cul-
ture unique to Japan; it exists everywhere in the world.
2. N である
"である" follows a noun to form the predicate of a noun-predicate sentence. It is similar to "です" and "だ".
However, "です" and "だ" are mostly used in spoken language, while "である" is used in plain-form written language. "である" also has a four-
quadrant conjugation, as follows:

① 東京は日本の首都である。／Tokyo is the capital of Japan.

② 信頼は大切である。／Trust is important.

5. 〜という N
When you need to refer to a person or thing that you or the listener are not familiar with, you can use "という" to intro-
duce it. It can be translated as "(a N) called...".

① 日本には、キャラ弁という弁当もある。／In Japan, there is also a bento called "chara-
ben" (character bento).

② 葛飾北斎という人を聞いたことがありますか。／Have you heard of someone called
Katsushika Hokusai?

to explain the specific content of the noun N. For example:

that they don't like cold bento.

When a question word is followed by "でも", it expresses that the part indicated by the question word applies to any element, and the statement
holds true in all cases. It can be translated as "no matter..." or "any...".

7. 〜ようだ。＜conjecture＞
Pattern: V/A attributive form + ようだ。
N の+ようだ。
Earlier we learned "〜みたいだ" for expressing conjecture. A noun (when attached to a noun, "noun + の") or the
attributive form of an inflecting word followed by "ようだ" can also express conjecture; it can be translated as "probably...", "it seems
...", etc. Like "〜みたいだ", "〜ようだ" expresses the speaker's inference based on their own impres-
sions or feelings. "〜ようだ" can also be used in written language or more formal situations, whereas "〜
みたいだ" is generally used more in spoken language.
ちゅうごくじん にほん つめ べんとう す
① 中国人は日本の冷たい弁当があまり好きではないようだ。／It seems that
Chinese people don't like cold Japanese bento very much.

② もうそろそろ時間のようなので、今日はこれで終わります。／It seems to be about time, so let's call it a day.

せん。／Tanaka seems busy lately. Even when I invite him to dinner, he won't come.
せんせい おこ
④ 先生は怒っているようでした。／The teacher seemed angry.
8. N のようだ。 ＜metaphor＞
"Noun + の" followed by "ようだ" is used for metaphor, equivalent to "like...". It is often paired with adverbs
such as "まるで" or "いかにも", and can be translated as "just like...".
にほん べんとう うつく げいじゅつひん
① 日本のお弁当は美 しくて、まるで芸術品のようです。／Japanese bento are beauti-
ful, just like works of art.
なかむら り ねえ
② 中村さんはやさしくて、李さんにとってお姉さんのようです。／Nakamura is kind, and to Li, she is like an older sister.
いっしゅうかん まいにちざんぎょう せんたくもの やま
③ この一週間は毎日残業 だったので、洗濯物がもう山のようです。
／I worked overtime every day this week, so the laundry is already piled up like a mountain.

9. こと <Formal noun>
Besides being used as an ordinary noun meaning "thing/matter", "こと" can also serve as a "formal noun":
when attached after the attributive form of an inflecting word, it functions to nominalize that word within the sentence. The formal noun
"こと" cannot serve as a sentence component on its own; it mainly performs a grammatical function in the sentence and is also often used
in certain fixed grammatical patterns. Other formal nouns commonly used in Japanese include "の" and "もの".
わたし ゆめ いしゃ
① 私の夢は、医者になることです。／My dream is to become a doctor.
べんとうぜんたい み も つ あじつ かんが つく
② 弁当全体のバランスを見ながら、盛り付けや味付けを 考 えて作る
にほんとくゆう ぶんか
ことは、まさに日本特有の文化の一つである。／Looking at the overall
balance of the bento while thinking about how to arrange and season it — this is truly one of Japan's unique cultural traits.

Pattern: V masu-stem + すぎる

Ⅰ

Ⅱ
A verb masu-stem, an i-adjective with the final "い" removed, or a na-adjective stem followed by "すぎる" expresses that
an action, behavior, or state exceeds the normal degree or range. It can be translated as "too..." or "excessively...".

I might not finish it by next week.

I think, is that I drank too much last night.
