1. V（よ）うとする
The volitional form of a volitional verb followed by "とする" can express that the first or third person,
as the agent, shows signs of being about to perform an action.

① 一日で平仮名とカタカナを全て覚えようとしましたが、できませんで
した。／I tried to memorize all the hiragana and katakana in one day, but couldn't do it.

② 宮崎駿監督はこのシーンで、宮沢賢治の作品『銀河鉄道の夜』のワン
えが

③ 多くの人があの企業 に入ろうとしています。／Many people are trying to get into that company.

The volitional form can also take the negative "としない", expressing that the third person shows no sign
of being willing to perform the action.

2. Written-style continuation form of verbs
We learned to use the te-form of verbs for sequential continuation, as in sentence a. This form is mostly
colloquial; in written Japanese or formal settings, the masu-stem of the verb can also express sequential
continuation, as in sentence b.
りゅうがく とき ひる だいがく かよ よる はたら
a. 留学していた時、昼は大学に通って、夜は働いていました。／While studying abroad,
I attended university during the day and worked at night.
りゅうがく とき ひる だいがく かよ よる はたら
b. 留学していた時、昼は大学に通い、夜は働いていました。
Note that the masu-stem used as a written continuative is limited to sequential sentences and cannot
combine with the negative form (V ない); when the masu-stem is a single syllable (e.g. "来", "見", "出"),
き み で
only the te-form can be used for continuation (as in example ③); the existential verb "いる" must first
be replaced by "おる" before its masu-stem can serve as a written continuative (as in example ④).

3. N みたいだ ＜example; metaphor＞
"みたいだ" attaches to a noun and, like "ようだ" learned in Lesson 25, is used for similes.
"〜みたいだ" is often paired with the adverb "まるで" — "just like...; as if..."

かれ い こども
① 彼はどうしてあんなにわがままなことを言えるんですか。子供みたい
です。／How could he say something so selfish? He's just like a child.
かっこう えいが
② この格好、いいですね。映画のスターみたいですよ。／That outfit looks great —
like a movie star.
A noun followed by "みたい（だ）" can also cite another thing (often the subject) as an example that shares
the features of the thing before "みたい". For exemplification, the attributive form "〜みたいな" or the
adverbial form "〜みたいに" is usually used. For example:
がくせい ひと だれ
③ あの学生みたいな人は誰ですか。／Who is that student-looking person?
あき まなつ あつ
④ もう秋なのに、真夏みたいに暑い。／It's already autumn, yet it's hot like midsummer.

4. N のような・N のように ＜example＞
Besides the simile use from Lesson 25, "〜ようだ" can also give examples. Like "〜みたいだ",
when used for examples it modifies a following noun as "N のような", or a following verb/adjective
as "Nのように".
り にほん ご はな ひと
① 李さんのように日本語がペラペラに話せる人はなかなかいない。／Few people can speak Japanese as fluently as Li.
せいかだいがく だいがくじだい す
② 清華大学のようなキャンパスで大学時代を過ごしてみたい。／I'd love to spend my university years
on a campus like Tsinghua's.

8. N をもとに（して）
A noun followed by "をもとに（して）" expresses using the preceding content as a basis or source material
for a speech act or creative work — "based on...; drawing on...".
じぶん たいけん げんこう か ほう おも
① 自分の体験をもとにしてスピーチの原稿を書いた方がいいと思う。／
I think I should write my speech based on my own experiences.
ぶんせき あたら どうにゅう
② 分析データをもとに、 新 しいシステムを導入 することにしました。
／Based on the analysis data, we introduced a new system.
ふ じさん かたち
③ このドレスは、富士山の 形 をもとにしてデザインしたものです。／
This dress was designed with reference to the shape of Mt. Fuji.
9. N をしている
When describing that a person, animal, or object has a certain outward appearance, the form
"N をしている" can be used. Here the noun (N) is usually accompanied by a modifier describing
a concrete feature, for example:

① ちびまる子ちゃんは丸い顔をしています。／Chibi Maruko-chan has a round face.

へん かたち たてもの なに
