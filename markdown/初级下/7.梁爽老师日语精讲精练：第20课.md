Grammar Notes
1. Japanese verb aspect and its polite and plain forms
“V ている” belongs to the category of “aspect (アスペクト)” in Japanese grammar and expresses
the state of an action (such as its continuation or completion). Besides the negative of “V ている”, “V ていない”,
“V ている” can also combine with the past tense to form “V ていた” or “V てい
なかった”. Table 20 summarizes the polite and plain forms related to “V ている”.
Table 20: Four-quadrant comparison of polite and plain forms of “V ている”
Affirmative  Negative
Present/future — Polite: Vています。 Polite: Vていません。
Plain: Vている。 Plain: Vていない。
Polite: Vていました。 Polite: Vていませんでした。
Past
Plain: Vていた。 Plain: Vていなかった。
あさ くも
① 朝からずっと曇っている。／It's been cloudy since morning.
じしん とき わたし にわ そう じ
② 地震の時、私は庭の掃除をしていた。／When the earthquake struck, I was cleaning the yard.

④ 小さい時は、まだ日本語を習っていなかった。／When I was little, I

2. V ている ＜continuation of a result (V ている-II)＞
In Lesson 19 we learned the first use of “V ている”, expressing the continuation of an action.
This lesson continues with other uses of “V ている”. The second use: attached to instantaneous
verbs, it describes the continuation of the result after the action has occurred.

② ガラスの窓が割れています。／The glass window is broken.
あに けっこん
③ 兄は結婚しています。／My older brother is married.

4. （まだ）V ていない
The negative form of the durative “V ている” is “V ていない”; its polite form is “V ていま
せん”. It is often used with the adverb “まだ” and can be translated as “not (yet) ...”.
This pattern is mostly used as a negative answer to “もうV ましたか。” (“Have you already ...?”).
こうえんかい はじ
① A: 講演会はもう始まりましたか。／Has the lecture already started?
はじ いそ
B: まだ始まっていませんよ。急いでください。／It hasn't started yet. Hurry up.

ぶちょう でんわ
② A: 部長に電話しましたか。／Did you call the department manager?
B: いいえ、まだしていません。／No, not yet.
ろんぶん き
③ 論文のテーマをまだ決めていません。／I haven't decided on my thesis topic yet.

6. V てから ＜after ...＞
The verb te-form followed by “から” expresses the sequence of actions and can be translated as “after (doing) ...,
(then do) ....”
くち じょうほう しょうひん か
① 口コミ情報をチェックしてから、商品を買います。／I check the online reviews first, and then

に ほん ふ ろ はい からだ あら はい
② 日本でお風呂に入るときは、体を洗ってから、入ります。／In Japan, when taking a bath,
you wash your body first and then get into the tub.

③ 上司に報告してから、レポートを書きます。／After reporting to my boss, I write the report.
7. (third person) V たがる/N をほしがる
In previous lessons we learned “V たい” for wanting to do something (Lesson 18) and “N がほしいです” for wanting
something (Lesson 11), as well as the person restrictions on these two patterns. This lesson covers how
these two patterns are used with the third person.
When expressing a third person's desire to perform an action, the “V たがる” form is generally used.
“-たがる” is a derived verb from “-たい”. Note that with “V たがる”, the verb's object
is marked with “を” and cannot be changed to “が”.
あに
① 兄はあのレストランのシェフになりたがっています。／My older brother wants to become the chef of that restaurant.
おとうと あたら か
② 弟 は新しいカメラを買いたがっています。／My younger brother wants to buy a new camera.
“V たがる” expresses the subject's (third person) desire to perform an action; in contrast,
“N をほしがる” expresses the subject's (third person) desire to obtain something.
おとうと あたら
③ 弟 は新しいカメラをほしがっています。／My younger brother wants a new camera.
むらかみなつ き せんせい
④ ジョージさんは村上夏樹先生のサインをほしがっている。／George wants Murakami Natsuki's autograph.

Expression Expansion
1. わざわざ
The adverb “わざわざ” means “to go out of one's way (to do something).” In this lesson, “わざわざ” carries
the nuance of “bothering to do (something unnecessary).”
か
① どうしてわざわざカメラを買うのですか。／Why go out of your way to buy a camera?
くるま じ かん げんていしょうひん か き
② わざわざ 車 で 5時間もかけて限定 商 品を買いに来た。／(They) drove 5 hours
out of their way just to buy the limited-edition item.
“わざわざ” is also used to comment on others' actions with the positive nuance of “taking the trouble to”, and is often paired with honorific
expressions. In this usage, it cannot be used by the speaker about their own actions. For example:
③ わざわざありがとうございました。／Thank you for going to all that trouble.
④ わざわざすみません。／Sorry to have made you go to all that trouble.
