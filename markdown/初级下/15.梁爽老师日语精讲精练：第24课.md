Grammar Notes
1. Verb potential form
The verb potential form expresses "can (do something)". The "can" expressed by the Japanese potential form
is close to "can" in English, and likewise expresses two meanings: (1) someone has a certain
ability, or (2) the conditions for doing something are met.
The conjugation rules for the verb potential form are as follows:
Type I verbs: change the final u-row kana to the corresponding e-row kana, then add る.
For example:
かえ かえ
返す：かえす→せ＋る＝返せる
か か
書く：かく→け＋る＝書ける
およ およ
泳ぐ：およぐ→げ＋る＝泳げる
ま ま
待つ：まつ→て＋る＝待てる
よ よ
読む：よむ→め＋る＝読める
あそ あそ
遊ぶ：あそぶ→べ＋る＝遊べる
おく おく
送る：おくる→れ＋る＝送れる
Type II verbs: drop the final kana る, then add られる. For example:
や や
辞める：やめる＋られる＝辞められる
ね ね
寝る：ねる＋られる＝寝られる
Group III (irregular) verbs:
① Suru-verb: change する to できる. That is:

2. 〜ところだ。
As a concrete noun, ところ can be written with the kanji 所 and mainly denotes a place. ところ
can also be used as a formal noun after various affirmative forms of verbs, followed by the copula だ, to ex-
press the stage the action is at.
a．V るところだ。
The dictionary form followed by ところだ indicates that the action is about to begin at the moment of speaking. Can
いま
be translated as "(just) about to ...". It is often paired with time adverbs such as 今, ちょうど, これから.

6. （N に）V てほしい / V ないでほしい
When the te-form of a verb is followed by "てほしい", it expresses the speaker's wish for the listener or someone else to do something; when the
nai-form is followed by "ないでほしい", it expresses the speaker's wish that the other person not do something. If you need to specify
whom you wish to do (or not do) the action, mark the agent of the action with the particle "に".

A (i-adjective) plain form + みたいだ。
Ⅰ
