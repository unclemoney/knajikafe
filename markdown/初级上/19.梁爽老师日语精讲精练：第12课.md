3. N なので、〜 <cause-effect −2>
The particle “ので” connects two clauses in a cause-effect relationship. In this pattern, when the first clause is a
“noun-predicate sentence” (see Lesson 5, Grammar 1), it can be joined directly in the form “N+な＋ので”;
this pattern can be translated as “because (it is) ...N..., ...”. Compared with “から”
from Lesson 9 (see Lesson 9, Grammar 4), “ので” tends to emphasize an objectively existing reason, and its tone

6. 〜の（／ん）です。 <explanation −1 (adjectives, nouns)>
Pattern: N + な + の（／ん）です。
A ＋の（／ん）です。
Ⅰ
A ＋な＋の（／ん）です。
Ⅱ
The pattern “〜の（／ん）です。” is very common in spoken Japanese; it can follow the “attributive form of an adjective”
or “noun + な”; in speech, the form “〜んです。” is mostly used. It expresses
a personal assertion, or an explanation of a situation. “〜の（／ん）です。” is hard to translate directly and is generally left untranslated. The specific functions and use
scenarios of the pattern “〜の（／ん）です。” are summarized below:
(1) Explaining a situation or giving a reason
Used to explain the “cause or reason” of what was stated before; mainly for the speaker's “explanation” of their situa-
tion. For example:

じつ つばさ おう じ さま す
① 実は『キャプテン翼』も『テニスの王子様』も好きなんですよ。／Actually, I love both Captain
Tsubasa and The Prince of Tennis.
いま いそが
② すみません、今はちょっと 忙しいんです。／Sorry, I'm a bit busy right now.
わたし つめ の もの にがて
③ 私は冷たい飲み物が苦手なんです。／I don't handle cold drinks well.
(2) Acceptance and realization

<!-- Page 8 -->

The situation described in the preceding text becomes the reason for the following “〜の（／ん）です”
sentence; the pattern “〜の（／ん）です” expresses the speaker's “acceptance” of that result,
with a sense of acknowledging and realizing the validity of one's own inference.
ゆ か ほんとう まんが
④ 由加さんは本当に漫画ファンなのですね。／Yuka, you really are a manga
fan.
(3) Stating the background of a topic, for indirect suggestions or requests
You can use “〜の（／ん）です” to state the background of a topic in order to make an indirect sug-
gestion or request. Here, “〜の（／ん）です” is mostly connected with the situation to be stated
afterward. This usage mostly appears in the form “〜の（／ん）ですが、〜”.
⑤ 新しいパソコンがほしいのですが。／I'd like to buy a new computer...
⑥ マックの最新モデルがいちばん軽いのですが。／The latest Mac model is the lightest...
right away.

⑦ あのう、テレビの音が大きいのですが。／Um, the TV is a bit loud...
Example ⑤ is a common expression when describing your specific needs to a shop assistant while shopping.
Example ⑥ uses the pattern “〜の（／ん）です” to explain to the other person, thereby imply-
ing “how about considering this one?”. Example ⑦ likewise indirectly explains the situation to express the speaker's implied request: could you turn the volume down?
