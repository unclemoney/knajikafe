Japanese pronunciation rules (4): geminate consonants (sokuon)
Japanese is a language that places great importance on mora timing: in Japanese words, each kana takes one beat. The geminate consonant is written with the small
“っ” (katakana “ッ” in loanwords). The geminate is not pronounced in the word, but it takes up one
beat of rhythm; therefore, when you encounter a geminate in a word, you need to pause for one beat before pronouncing the next sound. In romaji, geminates
ろっぽんぎ
are written by doubling the consonant letter after the geminate, e.g. roppongi (六本木).

Japanese pronunciation rules (5): long vowels / long contracted sounds
The long-vowel part of a Japanese word should be pronounced by lengthening the sound to two beats. How can you tell long vowels in
words? Generally follow these rules:
【a-row】kana + “あ” おかあさん（お母さん②）
【i-row】kana + “い” おにいさん（お兄さん②）
【u-row】kana + “う” すうがく（数学⓪）
【e-row】kana + “い” (or “え”) せんせい（先生③）
おねえさん（お姉さん②）
【o-row】kana + “う” (or “お”) おとうさん（お父さん②）
おおい（多い①）
Loanwords: mostly written with the long-vowel mark “ー” ビール①
When writing long vowels in Hepburn romaji, a macron “-” is generally placed over the long vowel letter, e.g. k
こうぞう
ōzō (構造), kēki (ケーキ), etc.

Japanese pronunciation rules (6): word combinations
Although each Japanese word has its own pitch accent, when two nominal words (mostly Sino-Japanese words) combine into
a new compound noun, the pitch accent of both words usually changes: the first
word of the compound is read with pattern ⓪, and the second word with pattern ①.

Common greetings (4)
1. さようなら。④⑤ Goodbye.
This phrase is not often used in daily life, because it is mostly used when parting with someone for a long time.
しつれい
2. 失礼します。② Excuse me. / I'll be going now.
This phrase is fairly polite and formal. It is mostly used before ending an interaction, such as when hanging up the phone,
or when finishing a visit and preparing to leave.
3. ありがとうございます。② Thank you.
“ございます” expresses respect. When thanking someone close to you, such as family or friends,
you can just say “ありがとう”.
4. おはようございます。⑧ Good morning.
Here too, it expresses respect and politeness. With people close to you, such as family or friends, you can just say “お
はよう”。
5. いただきます。⑤ (said before eating) Thank you for the meal.
Before starting a meal, Japanese people first say “いただきます” as a courtesy. This phrase originates from gratitude
toward those who provided the food, or toward the gods and Buddhas.
ちそうさま
6. ご馳走様でした。⑦ (said after a meal) Thank you for the meal.
Used after a meal.
