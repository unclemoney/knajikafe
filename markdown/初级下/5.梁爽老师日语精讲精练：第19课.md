Grammar Notes
1. V て (verb second conjunctive form)
Adding “て” to a verb forms the “verb te-form”; in Japanese school grammar it is also called the “verb second conjunctive form.”
The rules for changing a verb when followed by “て” are as follows:
Group I (godan) verbs:
The final kana of the verb changes according to the following four rules:
① For verbs ending in “う”, “つ”, or “る”, change “う”, “つ”, “る” to “っ”,
then add “て”. For example:
つか つか
使う：つかう→っ＋て＝使って
ま ま
待つ：まつ→っ＋て＝待って
おく おく
送る：おくる→っ＋て＝送って
② For verbs ending in “く”, change “く” to “い”, then add “て”. For example:
か か
書く：かく→い＋て＝書いて
およ およ
泳ぐ*：およぐ→い＋で＝泳いで
*Verbs ending in “ぐ” are followed by “で”
③ For verbs ending in “す”, change “す” to “し”, then add “て”.
はな はな
話す：はなす→し＋て＝話して
④ For verbs ending in “む”, “ぶ”, or “ぬ”, change “む”, “ぶ”, “ぬ” to “ん”,
then add “で”.
の の
飲む：のむ→ん＋で＝飲んで
よ よ
呼ぶ：よぶ→ん＋で＝呼んで
し し
死ぬ：しぬ→ん＋で＝死んで
い い
*The te-form of “行く” is irregular: “行って”
Group II (ichidan) verbs:
Drop the final kana “る” of the verb, then add “て”. For example:

5. V てください。 ＜request＞
The verb te-form followed by “ください” expresses a request for someone to do something, equivalent to “please ....”

Expression Expansion
1. こっち
“こっち” is the colloquial form of “こちら”, meaning “my side / this way”, “this one”, etc. Like-
wise, there are also “そっち”, “あっち”, and “どっち”.
2. もしもし
A set phrase when making a phone call, equivalent to “hello?” Besides “もしもし”, when answering the phone you can
also say “はい” followed by identifying yourself — your surname, or the name of your workplace or department,
etc.
ひさ
3. お久しぶりです。
Means “long time no see.”
はくぶつかん けんがく たの
4. 博物館の見学、楽しんでくださいね。
たの
Means “Enjoy your museum visit!” “〜を楽しんでください” means “please enjoy
...” / “have fun.”
5. では、またね。
This is an expression used between close acquaintances when saying goodbye; the tone is informal and relaxed. It means “see
you.”
あした
You can also add the date of the next meeting after “また”, e.g. “では、また明日ね” (“well, see you tomorrow”).
Young people in Japan today also shorten “では、またね” to “じゃね”, but this expression
should only be used between friends; it is inappropriate toward superiors or people you are meeting for the first time in formal settings.
